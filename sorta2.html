Rethinking food waste through simple, daily action.

2025 | New enterprise project

Sorta


About

I noticed that even when people want to dispose of food waste correctly, it often isn’t simple or automatic in everyday life.

I designed Sorta, a food sorting system and companion app, to explore how clearer information and reduced decision fatigue can make food disposal more impactful to encourage its use.

Role UX Research, Service Design
Timeline 8 weeks
Practice Academic | Individual Project
Tools Figma, Illustrator, VSCode
Explore the process ↓


Challenge

I was tasked with creating and pitching a human-centred social or commercial enterprise in Auckland that uses design to solve a real-world problem and enhance social, cultural, or community well-being.

Challenge

  • Food waste often happens in everyday routines without much thought
  • Sustainable intentions don’t always translate into consistent action
  • Existing systems don’t always make the right choice feel simple or visible

Solution

  • A system that encourages clearer everyday disposal decisions
  • Gentle prompts that support better habits over time
  • Designed to fit naturally into daily routines

DISCOVER

My curiosity towards climate action and how everyday behaviours influence environmental impact led me into further academic research, where I found key statistics in Auckland that,

Main insight from research

There is a lack understanding of the food waste system and food scrap bins feel unnecessary and more food ends up in landfill.

DEFINE

But... why?

I wondered: what was there a lack of understanding of? And why does it make it feel unnecessary?

So I started closer to home, looking at my own habits first, then speaking to and observing people my age in Auckland to understand how these behaviours actually play out day to day.

So... people do know they should sort. But they struggle with actually using their food scrap bins because of inconvenience and not seeing their impact.

And the new question turned into:

How might we...

Make food scrap bin benefits more visible in everyday life?



For environmentally aware young adults living independently in Auckland


The new scope of the project became:

Challenge

  • People struggle to understand where food scraps actually go
  • Bin systems feel inconvenient and easy to ignore
  • Awareness of impact is low in everyday disposal moments

Solution

  • Clearer guidance at the point of disposal
  • Reduced friction in everyday sorting decisions
  • Small moments of feedback to reinforce correct behaviour

CLARIFY

After, proposing the solution, and did extensive research on competitors with similar products.

An example of how the competitor, Kitro, uses the idea

To further understand where I can position this in the market, I did further case studies and SWOT analysis on competitors ( kitro , winnow solutions , lasso ) with similar products.

I saw an opportunity to simplify the concept for the target audience and applied key features to my solution.


The purpose and features for the service became clear; I was able to define key features and goals for the service that would set it apart in the market.

Large-scale focus ❌

Designed for restaurants and commercial waste systems, not everyday users

Too complex ❌

Heavy systems built for tracking data, not simple daily decisions

Low visibility ❌

Little feedback for individuals on their personal impact

Scaled for everyday use ✅

Translated system thinking into a simple, personal bin + app experience

Simplify process ✅

Reduced complexity into clear, low-effort actions at the point of disposal

Personal context ✅

Designed around individual daily routines rather than large-scale waste systems

REFLECTION

I thought...

this project made me realise I’m more interested in understanding people than designing interfaces. I became more drawn to the thinking behind user behaviour. What people do, why they do it, and how those patterns can inform better design decisions. Instead of focusing purely on the final output, I found myself valuing the process of research, observation, and uncovering insight.
